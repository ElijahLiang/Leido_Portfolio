Design Manifesto · Torch of Light

The meaning of life is how we illuminate one another.

Design, technology, and art are the torches I carry to reach people who have been overlooked and to meet them with warmth.

"Five years ago, under the lanterns of Nanjing's Confucius Temple, I made a wish: become a designer who can bring happiness to others."

— Night in Qinhuai, 2019

Since childhood I have been moved by how many gentle souls carry pain, how fragility and resilience coexist. I keep asking: how can design help the unseen be equally seen, and the unheard be gently understood?

Five years of practice taught me this: happiness cannot be given—it can only be empowered. My work builds spaces, tools, and systems that let people rediscover their own strength.

I hope every life—human or non-human—finds a space within my work to feel gently seen, kindly treated, and free to glow.

✉️ Letter to GDAT Admissions Committee

Nanyang Technological University · School of Art, Design and Media

Dear GDAT Admissions Committee,

Thank you for giving me the opportunity to provide additional clarification. My previous response was too brief and failed to fully convey my true thoughts.

I imagine that upon reviewing this material, you may wonder: Why is this applicant choosing NTU? Why would he consider leaving his current master's program to apply for GDAT? Beyond what I wrote in my Statement of Purpose, the following is a more detailed explanation of my circumstances and motivations.

I. Reasons for Withdrawing from My Current Graduate Program

1. Curriculum Mismatch with Professional Development Goals

I am currently enrolled in the MFA program in Digital Media Arts (Game Design Track) at Shanghai University of Engineering Science. However, Korean language is a required course worth 4 credits, which has no relevance to my professional development. More critically, the university lacks English academic writing courses, which has caused me significant difficulty in writing English papers. Additionally, the curriculum emphasizes theoretical instruction with limited practical opportunities—the only courses directly related to game development are my supervisor Prof. Zhang Yingfang's Unity course and Prof. Myung Ho Sung's scene design course. I am deeply grateful to both professors for writing my recommendation letters, especially my supervisor Prof. Zhang, who gave me her full support upon learning of my intention to apply to the GDAT program.

2. Insufficient Academic Resources and Support

Since 2023, I have been closely following developments in the AI field and have independently completed all the projects in my portfolio—from shader works, to the web prototype of "Be Happy Elijah," to the Embodied Agent game "Yangguang Series." These projects, from design and art to technical implementation, were all completed by me alone, without any resources or personnel support. At the same time, I had to cope with the heavy academic pressure at my university, including extensive Korean homework, political theory courses, and various lectures.

I have always hoped to conduct academic research using my proposed Open Theatre Theory as the theoretical foundation for Agent game development. However, the reality is: no one at my university can guide thesis writing in the AI/Agent field, no one around me understands this area, and I have even faced skepticism and ridicule from others. The more practical dilemma is: publishing papers requires self-funding expensive page fees and even agency fees, while the university's graduation requirements mandate publishing in low-impact Chinese SSCD journals—this contradicts my academic aspirations. The university lacks relevant laboratories and equipment support, and many experimental projects in my portfolio (such as locally deploying VLA models) had to be discontinued due to insufficient equipment.

My long-term goal is to pursue a doctoral degree, and frankly speaking, my current university's platform resources and academic reputation are not conducive to this plan.

II. Reasons for Choosing NTU's GDAT Program

1. Curriculum Highly Aligned with My Research Direction

The GDAT program's curriculum is highly consistent with my personal aspirations:

Game Theory and Social Games: This is precisely the direction in which I have always hoped to apply Yangguang NPC. I am passionate about serious game development—when I was interning at iFlytek in 2023 and witnessed virtual humans interacting with users, I found my life's mission: to help others through my work. I believe that in this course, I can discuss and refine my Open Theatre Theory in depth with professors.

Human-Computer Interaction: Before studying game design, I worked in UX design at iFlytek. This experience allowed me to integrate design methodology into my game development. I look forward to summarizing my past experience in this course, exchanging ideas with classmates and professors, while filling gaps in my knowledge, such as how to quantify users' emotional responses.

Virtual Reality and Neural Rendering / AI-Driven Computer Graphics: I have been self-studying shader programming and linear algebra, but always in a project-driven rather than systematic manner. I am familiar with NeRF and 3DGS technologies, but could not invest sufficient time to study them in depth due to academic pressure. If I can enter NTU, I would be thrilled to integrate my self-taught knowledge with systematic coursework.

2. A Clear Long-term Vision

I hope to deepen my work in serious games and Embodied Agent research at NTU, allowing my theories and projects to truly flourish. If given the opportunity, I very much hope to pursue a doctoral degree at NTU and publish a paper that could be accepted by CHI. I think I will consult more with Assoc Prof Davide Benvenuti and Assoc Prof Bernhard Johannes Schmitt, and with their help, I will realize my vision. Especially with Associate Professor Bernhard Johannes Schmitt, my next plan is to get my embodied agent running in the physical world.

III. Final Words

To be honest, this is my first time applying to an overseas institution, and I have no prior experience. I am that kind of person who doesn't good at expressing himself. NTU is the first and only university I have applied to. When you asked me how I would handle my degree situation, I turned to AI for guidance on how to respond—but I realized that I should share more authentic content with you.

The GDAT program is something I have been following since its establishment in the summer. It has been my North Star on the path of independent development, the motivation that sustained me through countless sleepless nights and helped me withstand the pressure of coursework and miscellaneous tasks, and the dream I held in my heart each time I stayed overnight in the office. I once visited the entrance of the ADM building and took a photo there, but was unable to go inside—I hope this time I can truly walk through those doors.

From the perspective of realistic academic pursuits and interest alignment, NTU's GDAT program is more suitable for me than CMU or USC. It was precisely because I chose to apply to GDAT that I became a better version of myself during the preparation process. I firmly believe that if I can enter NTU, my projects can take root here, blossom, and bear fruit.

Thank you for the efficiency and enthusiasm the program team has shown throughout our correspondence, and I sincerely apologize for any inconvenience caused by my previous response.

Respectfully yours,

Liang Yibin

Application: C2604402
