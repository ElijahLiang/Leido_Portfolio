🧩 01｜Research

“From Waste to Wisdom — discovering life’s hidden recyclers.”
We began by tracing the plastic crisis back to its smallest participants — mealworms. Their quiet not driven by machines, but by living systems. This research phase mapped the intersection of ecology, science, and design, laying the groundwork for a household-scale symbiosis between humans and nature.

🧠 02｜User Research

“Listening to hesitation, designing for empathy.”
Through interviews, empathy maps, and field studies, we explored how people emotionally react to insects — curiosity entangled with discomfort. These insights revealed that the challenge is not only technological but also psychological. To design for coexistence, we must first design for acceptance. Every hesitation became a guide, shaping a system that educates gently and connects emotionally.

🌱 03｜Prototype & Hardware

“Turning ecological theory into touchable life.”
The prototype transforms research into reality — a compact, transparent habitat where users can observe the entire lifecycle of mealworms as they process plastic. Every curve, vent, and texture is designed for both function and fascination. By transforming a biological process into an aesthetic and educational experience, the prototype bridges science and daily life, turning sustainability into ashared act of care.

📱 04｜Application Design

“Designing a digital ecosystem of empathy.”
The app connects people, worms, and knowledge through playful interaction. Users feed their worms, exchange insights, and witness the rhythm of a living system. Warm colors, rounded forms, and friendly icons translate ecological cycles into visual language. Here, sustainability becomes a game of kindness — one that teaches, rewards, and reminds us that every small gesture can sustain life.

🌿Reflection — Toward a Symbiotic Future

Redefining sustainability as emotional connection and shared learning.
Re:Cycle reminds us that every small action—feeding, observing, caring—can become an act of design. Beyond the data and prototypes, it’s a story about empathy: between species, between technology and life. Through playful design, we cultivate not only worms but also awareness, responsibility, and hope for a circular future.
